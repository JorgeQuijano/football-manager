## v0.40 — Light mode

The app shipped dark-only for thirty-nine versions. The theme is now System / Light / Dark via a single cycling icon button, resolved pre-paint from `localStorage` (`touchline-theme`) with the OS followed live only in System mode. `:root` holds the light palette; `[data-theme="dark"]` overrides it (the dark palette is unchanged). Amber/red/green accents that were hard-coded hex classes across 21 files became semantic tokens (`--warn`, `--danger`, `--positive`, `--*-soft`, `--*-line`).

## v0.41 — The ball follows the players

Match visuals were animated on their own track (the ball went to each slot's *formation coordinate*, at a fixed speed), which is why passes finished where nobody stood. The ball is now anchored to the motion bodies: live targets, distance-based durations, a `"feet"` phase that glues it to the carrier, and damped wander for whoever has it. Measured rest-gap to the nearest player: 0–1.1 units (was tens). The sim, timeline and goldens are untouched.

## v0.44 — The manager's career

The board judges results now, not just money: a confidence meter per league round, warnings that escalate (concerned → vote of confidence), a sacking that ends the run, a reputation that follows you, and job offers from other clubs in pre-season. Three design calls worth recording:

- **A sacking is game over.** There is no unemployed manager waiting by the phone — the career-over screen (reputation, record, clubs managed) is the epilogue, and "Start a new career" begins fresh. A job hunt from the outside was a bigger feature than the board itself, and there is no vacancy market to hunt in.
- **The target is the squad's station**, not a fixed ambition per club: the board ranks the twenty clubs by `squadValue` and asks you to finish about there. Sign superstars and the bar rises; sell the silver and it falls. One rule, read fresh — no per-club table to maintain.
- **Offers come from your own division, in pre-season only.** Moving abroad would re-key the fixture list and the world leagues' rosters; pre-season-only keeps the calendar untouched. The playbook (formation, customs, set pieces, training, scouts) travels with you; the club does not.

## v0.45 — Team styles, and a roll that cannot be gamed

Six playing styles as pure numbers in `tuning.ts` (`STYLES`): Balanced, Tiki-Taka, Direct, Gegenpress, Park the Bus, Counter — each a small set of orthogonal parameters (tempo, directness, risk, press height/intensity, line height, counter bias, retention, stamina, fouls, plus attack/defence multipliers). They are real inputs, not labels: a patient side strings more passes together, a direct side gets it forward, a pressing side runs itself into the ground and fouls more, a bus concedes the ball and the shots, and the side that wins it high gets a one-minute counter window. You pick yours in Tactics (`lineup.style`); every AI club has a deterministic identity from generation (`styleFor` from the club's id and strength band), backfilled by `normalizeSave` for old saves. Style-vs-style, the calibration harness (`scripts/sim.ts --stats`, `engine/analysis.ts`) shows the fingerprints: tiki-taka ~62% of the passes, the bus ~35%, gegenpress ~8 points of stamina heavier than the side it presses, and every style's chance rate matching its multipliers.

**The subtle bug worth recording.** The calibrated chance dice (`rng() < pH`) were drawn from the shared match stream. Because chain length depends on the style, the *position* in the stream at which each minute's roll landed correlated with the style — and mulberry32's local structure turned that correlation into a systematic bias: styles whose tempo differed from Balanced were silently getting up to **9% more chances than their multipliers**, and the deviation followed the style around (tiki +9%, gegenpress +8%, park-the-bus +6%) while direct and counter — tempo near 1 — looked clean. The instrument that caught it: summing the model's own `pH` per minute and comparing it with the realized chance count. With deterministic rolls the gap vanished, which pinned it on the stream. The rolls now come from their own stream keyed to the match seed and the minute (`hashSeed(flowSeed, m, "flowH"/"flowA")`), so a side's passing style can never bias anyone's chance rate — and style-vs-style comparisons now share the same roll sequence, which makes the harness itself a controlled experiment. After the fix, every style's realized chances match `ΣpH` to within noise over 200 matches.


## v0.46 — Possession is played, not narrated

The v0.45 note admitted the stopgap: the pass chain was still a random walk over formation coordinates, drawn *backwards* from a shooter chosen first. The passes looked plausible and caused nothing. This version replaces the walk with a possession model (`src/engine/positions.ts` + `src/engine/possession.ts`): every pass is played — a target is chosen from the live shape, and the ball is rolled against the passer's touch, the distance, the men on him and the bodies in the lane. If the move cannot reach its shooter, the chance dies: no shot.

**Six decisions worth recording.**

- **A move has its own dice.** The move's rolls come from `mulberry32(hashSeed(flowSeed, m, "move", side))` — its own stream, keyed to the match seed, the minute and the side, exactly the lesson v0.45 taught about the chance rolls. Nothing about one side's passing can shift anyone else's stream, and a move replays byte-identically.
- **The chance dice now know who has the ball.** The calibrated per-minute chance rolls (`baseChancePerMinute`) survive this version — they are the volume control until Stage 4 makes chances emerge from progression — but they are now weighted by the possession share: `pH ×= 2 × pPossHome`. A side that keeps the ball attacks more and its opponent attacks less. Without this, tiki-taka's patient build-up arrived less often than direct's quick one (43% vs 52% of chance moves reached the shooter) and the style was strictly worse; with it, tiki's extra possession pays for its slower final ball.
- **Where the ball was won is where the interceptor stands.** The old analysis read the interceptor's *formation* slot for the high-turnover stat — a defensive midfielder's template y, not the live spot. The model records `Stroke.ty` (the winner's frame, from the interceptor's real position), and `noteTurnover` now takes the live y, which also makes the counter window in `transitionEdge` honest. High turnovers went from a flat ~12/match for every style to 1.7 (bus) → 8.4 (gegenpress), which is what the style table always claimed.
- **A stalled move clears the ball.** When a move runs out of passes it ends with a ball played *forward* (the clearance the defence drops on), not a gift in the attacking side's own third — the reason deep turnovers stopped inflating every style's `wonHigh` equally.
- **The offside line is the live block, and attackers hold it.** The v0.42 rule read formation templates — and read them backwards: sorting the defending side's own-attacking-frame coordinates ascending made `men[1]` their second *forward*, not the second-last defender, and it compared depths from two different frames. It looked plausible only because templates are symmetric around y = 50. Now the line is the second-last man of the defending side's live `shapeFor` block at the moment of the pass (never past halfway), and the law compares the move's own points. The attack shape then *holds* that line, so the model stopped offering passes that were doomed to be flagged — the first version flagged 4.1 offsides a match and killed a fifth of all chances, because the striker stood twenty units beyond a mid-block. The remaining offsides come from the **early through ball** (`earlyBase + earlySpan·directness`): the striker is played in a step beyond the line, the flag goes up on the tight calls the assistant gets right, and the ones he misses are the VAR room's business. The goal path no longer re-rolls the flag — if the reception was missed, the goal stands unless `varReview × varCatch` catches it — which is what makes genuine overturns (and restorations) appear in the sample at all.
- **A perf pass after the model landed.** The twenty-club season test caught the bill: `Math.hypot` (overflow-safe and slow) on every distance, `toFrame` allocating a two-element array twenty-odd times per pass, and a `Map` rebuilt per pass in the candidate loop. `sqrt`, an inlined flip (`ysInOtherFrame`) and a reused weight array took the engine from 15.9 to 12.5 ms/match — the season test stays inside its 25 ms/match budget. The match view then moved to a lazy chunk: the initial bundle went from 269.8 to 254.1 KB gz, keeping the 270 KB requirement honest with room for Stage 4.

**Three geometry bugs found while calibrating**, all fixed in `positions.ts`:

- The press-engagement test was **inverted**: `engage = by < 45 + 42·pressHeight` meant Park the Bus pressed only in the opponent's half and dropped off in its own box, while the gegenpress engaged almost never. The sign is now `by > 60 − 50·pressHeight` (small `by` = the ball deep in their half = a high press).
- The defending block sat a fixed gap goal-side of the ball, so a deep ball put the bus's line on its own goal line. The gap now shrinks as the ball approaches (`min(lineGap, (100 − by)·0.6)`).
- Lane risk counted the man pressing the passer as a body *in the lane* (he stands 2.2 units off the ball), which saturated it at ~0.96. Defenders whose projection falls in the first quarter of the pass are now skipped — a man on your shoulder cannot intercept the ball played away from him.

**The numbers** (120 league matches, seed 42, `npm run sim -- --stats 120`): goals/match 2.55, shots 24.1 (xG 2.31), completion 82.8%, pass length short 9.2 / medium 56.3 / long 34.5, direction forward 48.8 / lateral 26.6 / back 24.6, turnovers won high 5.67/match, chain ends out 7.5 / turnover 58.0 / chance 17.8 / foul 15.6 / offside 1.1. Offsides run 1.2/match, with 5 overturned goals and 11 restorations across the 120-match VAR sample. Style fingerprints (24 seeds × 2 venues, style vs Balanced, at the final 0.18 dial): possession tiki-taka 63.3% vs direct 41.5% (21.8 points apart, target ≥12); gegenpress wins the ball high 8.25/match vs Balanced's 2.50 (3.3×, target ≥2×); tiki-taka 67.7% of passes under 20 units, direct 38.5% over 35 on this pairing and 40–43% on the engine-test fixture; completion tiki 92.1 / balanced 87.3 / direct 82.3; gegenpress finishes on 64.0 stamina against 71.6.

**Known and deliberate:** style *goal balance* is not yet level (tiki-taka and the bus still under-score; gegenpress over-scores) because the finishing layer converts every shot at the same rate regardless of how it was built. Distance- and situation-aware conversion is Stage 4's brief; this version's acceptance targets are about possession, pass mix, where the ball is won and the offside rate, and they pass — the one near-miss is direct's long share on the style-script pairing (38.5% against the 40% goal), which the engine-test fixture puts at 40–43%.

## v0.47 — Chances emerge from progression

The v0.46 note ended with the stopgap: the calibrated per-minute dice decided *whether* a chance move happened, and the shooter was chosen before the move was played. This version removes the dice. Every minute is one possession move; a completed pass into the shooting range (y ≤ 30) ends it as a shot, taken by whoever received it. Shot volume is now what the passing model produces — and the finishing layer finally knows where the shot came from.

**Six decisions worth recording.**

- **The shot is the end of a move, not a separate event.** `playMove` gains `shootRange`: men in the range carry the end boost on the final ball, and a completed pass into it returns `end: "arrived"` with the shooter as the last link of the chain. `resolveChance` (shooter chosen first) is deleted — `resolveShot` takes the move and the shooter the move found. The `flowH`/`flowA` dice streams and `baseChancePerMinute` are gone with it.
- **Distance-aware conversion.** `pGoal` is multiplied by `distF = clamp(1.25 − distGoal/42, 0.4, 1.15)` — the distance from the arrival point to the goal centre. A header from six yards and a 30-yard skid are no longer the same shot: xG/shot now sits at ~0.10 and tiki-taka's close-range arrivals are worth more than a direct side's hopeful ones. `conversionBase` rose 0.115 → 0.24 to keep the average shot worth what it was.
- **A move that is progressing keeps going.** A side that has crossed halfway when its budget runs out gets a second budget; a side that has not still dies with the clearance. Without this a short-passing side can never cross the pitch — tiki's moves were ending at y ≈ 50 with the strikers 20+ units ahead of the circulation. The other half of the fix is in the shape: in the final third the attacking midfielders now push *ahead* of the ball (the old `follow` crowded everyone behind it), so the short game has a man between the ball and the strikers' line.
- **Possession resumes where it was won.** The carrier is still drawn deep, but `fromY` (the winner's spot, from `lastTurnover`) bends the draw toward the man nearest it — a side that wins the ball in midfield builds from midfield.
- **Marking had to move into the move.** With the shooter pick gone, opposition instructions lost the channel that made them bite; `playMove` now weighs every receiver by `oiInvolve` (the OI's `involve` fold), compounded for the final-ball target, and the cross header pick carries it too. Measured over 80 fixtures: marking the two men the model actually uses costs them 26% of their shots and 33% of their goals, and the whole side 13% of its shots. The test's danger-man ranking was rewritten to match — advanced roles first, then shooting — because ranking by raw shooting+pace could single out a man the shape never puts near the range.
- **The AI and the assistant read danger the model's way.** `aiOppInstructions` now takes the opponent XI's roles and the assistant's threat tip reads `opp.roles`; both rank the danger man by role `shot + finish` first, then shooting and pace — the ranking the marking test settled on. Raw attributes marked men the shape never puts near the range, so the AI's plan was often spent on a ball-playing defender while the inside forward ran free. The AI's marking bites harder now: league goals 2.56 → 2.42 (still inside the 2.3–2.7 band), because the men who end moves are the ones being marked.

**The numbers** (120 league matches, seed 42, `npm run sim -- --stats 120`): goals/match 2.42, shots 24.1 (xG 2.35), completion 83.7%, pass length short 9.7 / medium 57.3 / long 33.0, direction forward 54.1 / lateral 23.0 / back 22.9, turnovers won high 5.67/match, chain ends out 9.1 / turnover 55.6 / chance 18.1 / foul 16.0 / offside 1.2. Offsides run ~1.2/match. Style fingerprints (48 seeds × 2 venues, style vs Balanced): possession tiki-taka 69.7% vs direct 39.2% (30.5 points apart); gegenpress wins the ball high 8.4/match vs Balanced's 2.6 (3.3×); tiki-taka plays 61.0% of its passes under 20 units, direct 36.6% over 35 on this pairing; completion tiki 91.8 / balanced 87.4 / direct 82.2. The gap this stage was meant to close: **tiki-taka's scoring deficit to Balanced is down to 1.02 vs 1.24 on the pairing** (v0.46: 1.04 vs 1.46, a 29% deficit). Engine-only cost ~11.8 ms/match.

**Known and deliberate:** the forward share is 54% (v0.46: 49%) — a consequence of chances emerging from progression: the model now steers the ball toward the box, and the plan's original 30–45% band described the old open circulation. Possession still does not survive the minute (every move ends in a turnover, an out or a shot), so territory never accumulates; the residual style gap — gegenpress 1.40 vs balanced 1.24 on the pairing, and a bus that concedes more than it deserves (1.83/match while scoring 1.03) — is where retention between moves would land. And the acceptance test's fixture sample runs colder than the league (tiki and gegenpress near 1.5 there), so its goals floor is wide; the league band 2.3–2.7 is the calibration source.

## v0.48 — The ball has a place

v0.47 ended with the gap named: possession did not survive the minute. The side that played the next minute was a fresh draw from `pPossHome`, so a side could win the ball on the edge of the box and be defending its own sixty seconds later. `MatchState.ballSpot` (`{ side, y, contest? }`) now carries the ball across minutes: every ending calls `giveBall`, and the next move resumes from that spot, with the man nearest it (the v0.47 `fromY` bend in `playMove`).

**Four decisions worth recording.**

- **A restart is firm, a live ball is contested.** A kick-off, a keeper's ball, a goal kick, a throw and a free kick are theirs outright — the next minute's move is played by that side, from that spot. A ball carried out of passes (`held`) and a ball won by an interception are *live*: the carrier keeps it with `keep = clamp(keepBase + 2(1−keepBase)(p_side−½), 0.5, 0.95)` — odds bent by the side's share prior, so the *sequence* is emergent while the *share* still converges to `pPossHome`, the volume control v0.45–v0.47 built everything on. Measured: live balls stay with their side ~78% of the time (the engine test holds 55–95%).
- **A rebound is not a possession.** The first cut ran the retention roll on every ball, and goals exploded to 3.42/match: a save or a block left the ball 5–15 units from goal, and 22% of them were handed straight back to the attacker for a point-blank rebound. Only a carry and a won ball are live; every shot outcome hands the *defending* side a firm restart (a keeper's ball at 95, a goal kick at 94, a block at 85, a cleared corner at 55, a kick-off at 50).
- **Every spot is written in the next side's frame.** `playMove` and its coordinates use the attacking frame (0 = the goal attacked, 100 = own goal), so `giveBall` values do too: a goal kick at 94 is *the keeper's* 94, and a throw at the ball's spot is `100 − move.last[1]` for the side that takes it. The frame audit caught the first draft placing a disallowed goal's free kick in the wrong half. The half now mirrors real football: the home side kicks off, the away side kicks off the second half.
- **The `held` ending.** A move that runs out of passes within `holdFrom` (45) of the goal may keep the ball instead of gifting a clearance — `hold = clamp(holdBase + holdSpan(retention−1), 0, holdMax)` — so tiki-taka camps and a bus clears. It is the only ending that is both new and live, and it stops every stalled move from being a free counter. `analysis.ts` reports it (`ends.held`) and the calibration report prints it.

**A measurement lesson, recorded because it cost a day.** An A/B over the usual harness showed the v0.48 finishing roll converting at 0.785 against its own xG — a 21% deficit that looked like a stream-alignment bug (v0.48 consumes one draw fewer per firm minute, so the goal roll samples a different draw). It was not: the harness reused the same 18 fixtures 22 times, and per-match conversion has a standard deviation of ~65% (each match rolls ~20 draws against their own `pGoal`). At 3000 unique fixtures the two arms agree: v0.48 0.975, v0.47 0.988 (0.7σ). The same artefact produced the "away skew" (120 fixtures: home 1.13 / away 1.51 → 2280 unique fixtures: 1.243 / 1.207) and the "gegenpress overreach". **The fixture sample is the unit of measurement**: every harness now varies the club pair (or the round) so each match is unique. The stream shift itself exposed two thin tests, both re-based on measured effect sizes: the tired-legs check now asserts on the user's xG (per-fixture SD 0.32 against an ~0.08 effect, ~3.7σ over 200 fixtures) instead of the goal difference (SD 1.22), and the foul-attribution check runs 60 fixtures (a 55% share to the pressing side, 3.9σ) instead of 12 (137–138, a coin flip).

**The numbers** (120 league matches, seed 42, `npm run sim -- --stats 120`): goals/match 2.63, shots 23.6 (xG 2.38), completion 83.4%, pass length short 9.4 / medium 56.9 / long 33.7, direction forward 54.1 / lateral 22.3 / back 23.6, possession home 50.3%, turnovers won high 5.51/match, chain ends out 9.7 / turnover 54.9 / chance 17.8 / foul 16.2 / offside 1.2 / **held 0.1**. The full double round-robin × 6 rounds (2280 unique matches): 2.45 goals/match, home 1.243 / away 1.207. Style fingerprints (200 unique fixtures, style vs Balanced): possession tiki-taka 67.0% vs direct 42.2% (24.8 points, target ≥12); gegenpress wins the ball high 9.52/match vs Balanced's 2.81 (3.4×, target ≥2×); tiki-taka plays 53.7% of its passes under 20 units, direct 39.0% over 35; completion tiki 92.1 / balanced 87.4 / direct 82.0; gegenpress finishes on 63.3 stamina. What continuity changed: possession moved to the sides that sit deep (the bus 35.8% → 42.2%, counter 39.2% → 41.4%) because a deep side now keeps what it wins instead of handing it back on the next coin flip; gegenpress's attack eased (1.33 → 1.10 goals against a balanced side's 0.98 → 1.04) and the bus conceded less (1.84 → 1.44) for the same reason — the goal spread narrowed, and the fingerprints, not the scorelines, are the style identity now.

**Invariants** (asserted in `engine.test.ts`, "possession continuity (v0.48)"): the ball always has a valid spot; a goal hands it to the side that conceded; the break hands it to the away side; every firm restart is played by the side that has it (checked minute-by-minute over six matches by reading the `pd` strokes' side); a live ball stays with its side between 55% and 95% of the time; the state round-trips through JSON and the half-time split reproduces the one-shot match exactly, `ballSpot` included. Cost stays ~12 ms/match.

**Known and deliberate:** the retention roll only decides *live* balls, so the possession share is still a prior, not a fact — a side can lose three live balls in a row and see none of them. `held` is rare by construction (0.1% of chain ends): it only fires when a move runs out of passes inside 45 units of goal, which is exactly where most moves prefer to shoot anyway. The style *goal* balance is narrower but not level (gegenpress still over-scores against a balanced side), and the calibration report's venue split at 120 fixtures is not to be trusted — the pairing is structurally unbalanced there; use 380+ fixtures or paired orientations.
